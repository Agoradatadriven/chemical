How the models were used

Each series — company-wide and per product — was fit on the 22-month monthly throughput history, then projected six months forward (Nov 2025 – Apr 2026)

SARIMA — Seasonal ARIMA

Configured as SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,0,0)₁₂: a non-seasonal ARIMA core plus a 12-month seasonal autoregressive term to capture year-over-year repeating patterns.

  • (1,1,1) — 1 autoregressive lag, 1 difference to remove trend, 1 moving-average term
  • (1,0,0,12) — links each month to the same month one year prior, so recurring annual peaks (e.g. mid-year production ramps) carry into the forecast
  • Best suited to the products with a full 22-month history showing repeating cycles

ARIMA — Non-seasonal

Configured as ARIMA(1,1,1): the same core as SARIMA but with no seasonal component, projecting purely from recent trend and momentum.

  • AR(1) — next value depends on the previous month
  • I(1) — one round of differencing makes the series stationary (removes drift)
  • MA(1) — corrects using the prior forecast error
  • Acts as the baseline against SARIMA: where the two diverge, seasonality is driving the difference

Why both, plus Moving Average & Exponential Smoothing: Moving Average (3-month) gives a flat, smoothed baseline; Exponential Smoothing (Holt, additive trend) weights recent months more heavily. Showing all four side-by-side lets the team see the forecast range and pick the model that best fits each product's behavior — short-history products lean on the simpler models, while stable long-history products are well-served by SARIMA.
